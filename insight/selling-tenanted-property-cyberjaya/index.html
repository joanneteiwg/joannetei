Selling a Tenanted Landed Home in Cyberjaya: What Owners Should Prepare

Cyberjaya Properties | Wolf Group

A Cyberjaya landed home with an existing tenant can appeal to an investor who wants rental income from the start. A buyer who intends to move in will need to know when they can take possession. Decide which buyers you can accommodate by reading the signed tenancy agreement before listing.

Start with the tenancy file

Keep the signed agreement and any extensions or side agreements together. Prepare a simple record of the current rent, last payment received, any arrears, deposits held, expiry date and any renewal rights. Check what the agreement says about viewings, notice and early termination. Share records through your agent or lawyer and remove tenant details from public marketing. Malaysian Bar: Selling your property

Which handover are you offering?

If the buyer wants…What to clarify before accepting
To take over the tenancyHow rent will be apportioned, how deposits are accounted for, when the buyer receives the tenancy records, and who notifies the tenant about payment instructions
To move inWhen the tenant can leave under the existing agreement or a separately agreed surrender, and how the sale contract will deal with vacant handover and delays

Ask your lawyer to draft the relevant Sale and Purchase Agreement terms for whichever arrangement the buyer accepts. Check what you can commit to before agreeing to a handover date. Malaysian Bar: Selling your property

Give both the tenant and the buyer a clear process

Arrange viewing windows with the tenant in line with the agreement. Confirm when photographs can be taken and who is allowed to attend. For an investor enquiry, share the rent and lease expiry together so the buyer can judge income and renewal timing. If there’s already an agreement for the tenant to leave, give the details to your lawyer and agent so they can discuss the handover accurately with buyers.

At completion, use a short handover list covering keys, access cards, the tenancy file, deposits, rent adjustments and management or utility paperwork that applies to the property.
