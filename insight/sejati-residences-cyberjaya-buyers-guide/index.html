Compare the same house type first

Get the original plan for each property and note the number of storeys, land dimensions, built-up area and whether it’s an intermediate, end or corner lot. Walk through any changes against the plan. A ground-floor space described as a bedroom may serve well as an office but have limited privacy or inconvenient bathroom access for someone using it overnight.

Walk around the property as well as through it

Visit the back and side boundaries to see what the windows face. Check how close the next house is and whether the outdoor area feels private enough for the way you plan to use it. If you’re buying for a family, try the walk from the house to the park. Ask for current information on management charges, facility access and visitor parking for the exact phase.

What’s a sensible basis for an offer?

Start with recent completed transactions of comparable homes in the same phase or collection, then compare competing listings of a similar size and lot position. If your preferred property costs more, identify exactly what the premium buys, such as a more usable side garden, a layout that suits your household or less work after you move in.
